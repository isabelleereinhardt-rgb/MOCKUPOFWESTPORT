# Westport 3D asset spec

This spec covers every model, render and data file that feeds the Westport 3D map (`index.html`) and the homes gallery (`homes.html`). Read it before building anything.

## Ground truth

- **Geometry:** `data/wpdata.json` is the Westport Atlas (https://claude.ai/artifact/PmUtdjnwRgDswxyJbfLC6D) geometry, in meters. Atlas x runs east, y runs north. Origin is Courthouse Square. Every building is a 4-corner oriented rectangle: `b[i] = [kindIndex, height*2, x1*2, y1*2, ... x4*2, y4*2]`. The kinds are `house duplex mobile shed barn shop office tower deck retail apartments townhouse clubhouse church school gym stands hospital industrial civic motel hotel`. Roads: `[nameIndex, classIndex, isBridge, [x, y, x, y, ...]]`, with classes `trunk primary secondary tertiary residential service track path motorway`. Addresses: `addr = [[buildingIndex, streetNameIndex, number], ...]`.
- **Gazetteer:** `data/gaz.json`, with `places[]` (`id, name, cat, status, at:[x,y], addr, hood, people, note`) and `people[]` (`name, group, color, role, home:{at, addr, status, note}`).
- **Register:** `docs/westport-register.md`, the author's canon. Status words matter: locked, Canon 2, your site, confirmed or proposed. Never present an interpretation as canon.
- **Site specs:** `docs/site-specs.json` holds each character home's Atlas footprint, street frontage, curb line and neighbors in the home's local frame (generated by `tools/qa/site-specs.py`).

## Model conventions (every GLB)

- Units are meters, at real-world scale.
- Build in Blender with +Z up and the **street-facing front facing -Y**. Local +X is to your right when you stand in the street facing the front door.
- The **origin is the center of the Atlas footprint rectangle, at finished grade (z = 0)**. Foundations, piers or skirting continue down to z = -0.6, so the model sits cleanly on gently sloped ground.
- Export with `wp.export_glb()` (glTF binary, +Y up, so the front faces +Z in the file). Do not use Draco or meshopt compression; the viewer loads plain GLB.
- Materials are Principled BSDF only, with image textures from the `wp.tex_*` generators (procedural, so there are no licensing questions). Do not download textures or models from the internet. Use at most 2048 px textures, and 1024 px for anything smaller than a house. Keep material count low; reuse materials across objects.
- Join objects by material where practical (`wp.join`), so a house is a handful of meshes, not hundreds.
- Run `wp.finalize(objs)` before export. It applies modifiers and box-projects UVs in meters, scaled by each material's tile.
- Never put a large lawn or ground plane in an exported GLB; the map draws its own ground. Hardscape (driveways, walks, patios) sits at z = 0.02 to 0.05.
- Name objects plainly: `House`, `Porch`, `Roof`, `Lot_Fence`, `Lot_Drive`, `Tree_LiveOak_1`.

## Shared toolkit

`tools/blender/lib/wp.py`. Import it from any script with:

```python
import os, sys
sys.path.insert(0, os.path.join(os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__)), '..', 'lib'))
import wp
```

It provides scene reset, textures (`tex_brick`, `tex_siding`, `tex_shingles`, `tex_metal`, `tex_wood`, `tex_ground`, `tex_grass`), `mat`, `glass`, geometry (`box`, `cyl`, `lathe`, `prism`, `roof_hip`, `array_along`, `join`, `uv_box`, `finalize`, `tri_count`), `export_glb`, and the render rig (`render_rig`, `camera`, `render`). Extend it in your own script. Do not edit `wp.py`, because other builders use it at the same time. Set `WP_TEX_DIR` to a private scratch folder when you run.

Run scripts with `python3 <script>`; Blender is installed as the `bpy` module (Blender 5.2). Cycles runs on the CPU. Use `threads=2` in `render_rig`, because two builders share four cores. Iterate at 32 samples and 800 x 500, and make finals at 128 samples and 1600 x 1000.

## Hero homes: the Nine

For each home, deliver:

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `tools/blender/homes/<id>.py` | The build script. It is self-contained apart from `wp.py`, and reproducible: running it rebuilds the GLBs and the renders. |
| `models/homes/<id>.glb` | The house, with porches, steps, chimneys and everything attached to it. |
| `models/homes/<id>-lot.glb` | Site dressing inside the lot: driveway, walks, fence, hedges, beds, mailbox, specimen trees and lot-specific props. |
| `models/homes/<id>.json` | Metadata (shape below). |
| `renders/<id>.jpg` | A front three-quarter view at eye level (about 1.7 m), 1600 x 1000, Cycles 128 samples, denoised. |
| `renders/<id>-aerial.jpg` | A 35 to 45 degree aerial of the house and lot, 1600 x 1000. |

Budgets: house plus lot up to 250k triangles and 12 MB combined. Monet's and the Delacroix house may use up to 350k and 16 MB. Every other home should aim for half of that.

Fit the site:
- Keep the main body within the Atlas footprint (`footprint_w_along_street_m` x `footprint_d_deep_m`). Porches and steps may reach up to 3 m beyond the footprint toward the street.
- The lot runs from the curb line (`curb_line_local_y`, negative, toward the street) to about 25 m behind the origin. Side to side it runs halfway to the neighboring buildings (`neighbors_local`). Keep every lot object inside that rectangle, with nothing crossing a neighbor's half.
- The driveway must reach the curb line exactly, so it meets the street the map draws.
- `atlas_height_m` is approximate. A hero home may differ from it by about 30% if the reference needs it; record the true ridge height in the JSON.

Metadata JSON:

```json
{"id": "monet-williams-house", "name": "Monet Williams's house", "address": "648 Oak Street", "neighborhood": "Garden District",
 "residents": ["Monet Williams", "Calista Yuen", "Victor Yuen", "Gordy Yuen"], "status": "Canon 2 (address); design from the author's photo",
 "atlas_building_index": 6973, "footprint_m": [23.5, 20.0], "ridge_height_m": 12.4, "stories": 2,
 "style": "Lowcountry Victorian farmhouse", "materials": ["painted lap siding, pale blue-grey", "..."],
 "triangles": 123456, "files": {"house": "models/homes/monet-williams-house.glb", "lot": "models/homes/monet-williams-house-lot.glb"},
 "lot_local_rect_m": [-21, -28.7, 21, 25], "notes": "anything the author should know"}
```

### Design briefs

Reference images are in `reference/` (local only, never committed). The author asked for realism above everything else: real proportions, real construction details, weathering where it belongs, and no toy look.

1. **monet-williams-house**, 648 Oak Street, Garden District (Canon 2). Calista lives here with her grandmother Monet, her dad Victor and her uncle Gordy. The family has been in Westport since the 1870s, and the house is kept up well. The design comes from `reference/monet-house.webp` (also `reference/board-18.jpg`), a Lowcountry Victorian farmhouse:
   - **Main body:** two stories, painted lap siding in pale blue-grey (about #B8C3CD), white trim and corner boards, and navy shutters (#2B3350) on every window.
   - **Roof:** a steep hipped roof in weathered grey architectural shingles, with three gabled dormers on the front, each with a 6-over-6 window and white trim.
   - **Porch:** a deep one-story wraparound porch across the front and around both sides, with a low hip roof. It has white turned posts with spindle brackets, a white balustrade with square balusters, and a beadboard ceiling.
   - **Balcony and entry:** a small second-floor balcony sits on the porch roof over the entry, with a white railing and flower boxes, reached by a French door. The entry is a pair of doors with a transom and sidelights, reached by brick steps (5 to 6 risers) between brick cheek walls.
   - **Foundation and wing:** the house is raised about 0.9 m on brick piers with white lattice between them. A one-story side wing on the left has its own hip roof. There are two brick chimneys.
   - **Lot:** a white picket fence along the front, with a gate at the walk; a brick walk from the gate to the steps; boxwood and azaleas along the porch; a rounded ornamental tree right of the walk; and two big live oaks with Spanish moss, one framing the front left and one behind the house on the left. A driveway runs along the right side.

## Archetypes (generic buildings, instanced across the town)

There are 25,281 houses, 1,474 duplexes, 3,128 mobile homes and 3,841 sheds, among others. The map draws far buildings itself, from footprints. Near the camera it places archetype models, scaled to each Atlas rectangle, so each one must scale gracefully.

- **Bounding box:** the model's full XY bounding box, including porches and steps, is centered on the origin. The app scales it non-uniformly so that the box equals the Atlas rectangle (x to the width along the street, y to the depth, z to the Atlas height), so record `bbox_m: [w, d, h]` exactly.
- **Materials:** use exactly these names, so the app can tint them per instance. `Wall` is siding, white or light grey, because instance color multiplies it. `Roof` is a mid-grey shingle or metal, also tinted per instance. Then `Trim` (white), `Glass`, `Door` (tinted) and `Masonry` (brick or block foundation, chimneys and steps). Use no other material names. If something needs a different look, give it its own archetype.
- **Budget:** at most 3,000 triangles for houses and 2,500 for mobile homes, so no 2048 textures; use 1024 px or flat colors. Model the depth that reads from 50 to 300 m away: roof overhangs, porches, window recesses, chimneys and steps.
- **Files:** `models/archetypes/<id>.glb` and an entry in `models/archetypes/index.json`: `[{"id", "kinds": ["house"], "bbox_m": [w, d, h], "stories": 1, "best_aspect": w/d, "neighborhoods": ["Riverside", ...], "triangles"}]`. The index is written by the builder of each group into its own file, `models/archetypes/index-<group>.json`, so builders never collide.

## Vegetation kit (`models/vegetation/`)

These are used tens of thousands of times as instances. Each species is two GLBs, `<id>.glb` (LOD0, at most 2,000 triangles; at most 3,000 for the live oak with moss) and `<id>-lod1.glb` (at most 300 triangles, matching silhouette and color). Each GLB has at most two materials, `Bark` and `Leaves`; vary color with vertex colors (`COLOR_0`), which the app enables. Model the canopies as clustered, lumpy, solid crown masses with darker interiors and undersides, which read as real trees from above and at an angle. Avoid single spheres and lollipop shapes. The origin is the trunk base and the heights are real.

Species:
- `live-oak` (16 m tall, 22 m spread, low wide limbs, with grey-green Spanish moss strands hanging from the limbs)
- `live-oak-young` (9 m)
- `magnolia` (11 m, dense dark glossy green, conical-oval)
- `loblolly-pine` (24 m, tall bare trunk, high open crown)
- `pine-young` (10 m)
- `crepe-myrtle` (5 m, multi-trunk, with a pink-flower variant `crepe-myrtle-pink`)
- `sabal-palmetto` (9 m)
- `bald-cypress` (18 m, for wet edges and the marsh)
- `hardwood` (a generic maple or sweetgum, 15 m)
- `shrub` (1.2 m), `hedge-1m` (a 1 m long, 1.1 m tall boxwood segment that tiles)
- `marsh-grass` (a 2 x 2 m clump of spartina)

Also deliver `models/vegetation/index.json` (`[{"id", "height_m", "spread_m", "triangles", "lod1_triangles", "use": "streets|yards|woods|wet|coast"}]`) and `renders/vegetation.jpg`, a lineup render.

- `water-tower` at 592 Mariner Drive (locked). A classic elevated steel tank on legs, about 45 m tall, pale blue-grey. The tank reads WESTPORT, with FOUNDED 1625 below it in smaller letters. The lettering is canon. Build the letters as real geometry (Blender text converted to mesh) in dark navy.
- `pier-bar` (locked), at the seaward end of "the pier and the pier bar" (376 Harbor Drive). The pier deck itself is drawn by the app from `wpdata.piers`. Deliver only the bar: a weathered one-story wood building on the pier deck, about 10 x 14 m, with a wraparound deck rail, a metal roof, string lights and a few tables. Record the pier polyline index it sits on, and its position as a distance along the pier.
- `williams-funeral-home`, 205 Main Street (locked; Monet's family business since after the Civil War). A dignified two-story downtown building in white-painted brick or stucco, with a columned porch and black shutters.
- `chadwicks`, 524 Harbor Drive (you confirmed). The town's one diner, on the downtown waterfront: a classic one-story diner with big windows, a counter visible inside, and an awning.
- `courthouse`, Lindon County Courthouse at 129 Second Street (Canon 2). A neoclassical brick courthouse with a white portico and a cupola with a clock.
- `carnegie-library` (1912), 285 Third Street. A small Beaux-Arts library with a pedimented entry and a stone base.

## QA, alignment and distances (data)

- `tools/qa/audit.mjs` writes `data/qa.json` and `docs/QA-REPORT.md`. Format: see the header of `audit.mjs`.
- `src/roadgraph.js` is the shared road graph and router, which both the browser and Node can import. `tools/dist/distances.mjs` writes `data/distances.json`, `data/distances.csv` and `docs/DISTANCES.md`.

## Rules for every builder

- Write only the files your brief names. Do not touch `src/`, `index.html` or `homes.html`, another builder's files, or `wp.py`. Do not run git.
- No text or logos on models, except the water tower lettering. Invent no business names or signs.
- Verify your own work. Render it, look at the render with the Read tool, compare it with the reference image, and fix what is off (proportions first, then materials). Do at least two passes before the final render.
- Report what you made, the triangle counts and file sizes, what you interpreted versus what came from a reference, and anything you could not do.
